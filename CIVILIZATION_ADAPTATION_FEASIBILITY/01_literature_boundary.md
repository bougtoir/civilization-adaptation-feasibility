# 01 — Literature boundary and novelty assessment

Working title: "When civilization constrains adaptation"

## Search scope

Searches covered: lock-in (infrastructure, technological, carbon, policy, adaptation), path dependence,
rigidity/poverty traps in social-ecological systems, transformational adaptation, adaptation pathways,
maladaptation, stranded assets, managed retreat / planned relocation, climate migration, crop switching,
food-system resilience, civilizational collapse (HCS literature), Seshat / CrisisDB programme,
historical persistence (Roman roads, settlement continuity), niche construction, cultural evolution,
sunk-cost effects at societal scale. Target venues per brief: Nature, Science, PNAS, NCC, Nature
Sustainability, NHB, GEC, One Earth, ERL, Climatic Change, World Development, JAS, Seshat-related work.

## Where the hypothesis already exists conceptually

The proposition "structures accumulated through successful past adaptation reduce flexibility under
changed conditions" is anticipated in four places:

1. **Rigidity-trap theory** (Carpenter & Brock 2008; Allison & Hobbs 2004; Cinner & Ennis lineage in
   SES resilience work). The mechanism is identical in spirit — self-reinforcing accumulation erodes
   adaptive capacity — but exists as formal models and single-region narratives, never as a
   cross-societal empirical test.
2. **Lock-in literatures** (Unruh 2000; Seto et al. 2016; David 1985). Establishes that accumulated
   capital/institutions constrain transition. The dependent variable in this literature is emissions or
   technology persistence, *not* a society's transformational flexibility under environmental change.
3. **Transformational adaptation / maladaptation** (Kates et al. 2012; Juhola et al. 2016; Wise et al.
   2014). Defines the outcome class but does not test what predicts it.
4. **HCS empirical programme** (Degroot et al. 2021; CrisisDB / Navigating Polycrisis). Documents
   diversity of societal responses to climate shocks and warns about biases; has not published a pooled
   environmental-change × legacy-structure interaction on a transformation outcome.

## What is NOT established

No located study:
* estimates an interaction between independently-measured environmental change and a pre-shock measure
  of legacy dependence on a transformation outcome (as opposed to persistence or decline);
* tests the context-dependent **sign reversal** of inherited capital (asset → liability) that the
  strong version of the hypothesis proposes;
* joins the historical/archaeological layer to the contemporary and counterfactual layers as one
  general mechanism.

## Novelty classification

**B = incremental but potentially defensible.**

The conceptual core (rigidity trap / lock-in) is ~20 years old and well cited; a reviewer can name it.
The defensible novelty is therefore *not* the mechanism but the **empirical proposition**: a
large-N, cross-domain test that accumulated adaptive capital moderates the environment→transformation
relationship, including a falsifiable sign-reversal prediction. The paper must cite the trap/lock-in
literature as the theory it operationalizes and must not claim a new concept. Framing the result as
"a general empirical test of adaptation lock-in across civilizational and contemporary systems" is
defensible; framing it as discovery of a new principle is not.

## Gate-1 consequence

Proceed to data feasibility audits. The literature search does not return C (substantially
pre-existing) because the specific empirical claim — EnvChange × LegacyDependence →
TransformationalFlexibility, tested across independent domains — remains unoccupied.
